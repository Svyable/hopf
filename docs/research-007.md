# Research 007 — Linked fibers, Villarceau tori and the Hopf invariant

Conventions follow notebook 004: z = (z₁, z₂) = (x₀ + i x₁, x₂ + i x₃) ∈ S³, the Hopf map h(z) = (2 Re(z̄₁z₂), 2 Im(z̄₁z₂), |z₁|² − |z₂|²), and stereographic projection σ(x) = (x₀, x₁, x₂)/(1 − x₃) from the pole (0, 0, 0, 1). The base point with colatitude θ and azimuth φ is [z₁ : z₂] with z₂/z₁ = tan(θ/2) e^{iφ}; its fiber is {e^{it}(cos(θ/2), sin(θ/2)e^{iφ})}, oriented by increasing t.

## 1. Fibers are Villarceau circles of nested tori (exact)
The fibers over colatitude θ fill the torus T_θ = {|z₁| = cos(θ/2), |z₂| = sin(θ/2)}. The pole has z₁ = 0, so rotations z₁ ↦ e^{iα}z₁ fix it and act on ℝ³ as rotations about the Z axis: σ(T_θ) is a surface of revolution. In a meridian half-plane write z₂ = sin(θ/2)e^{iβ}; then ρ = cos(θ/2)/(1 − sin(θ/2) sin β) and Z = sin(θ/2) cos β/(1 − sin(θ/2) sin β). This is a circle (an inversion of one), with extreme radii cos(θ/2)/(1 ∓ sin(θ/2)). Hence σ(T_θ) is a torus of revolution with

  R = sec(θ/2),  r = tan(θ/2),  R² − r² = 1.

The core circle (θ → 0) is the unit circle in the XY plane, and the fiber over the south pole passes through the pole and is the Z axis. Stereographic projection maps circles to circles, so each fiber is a round circle on σ(T_θ) winding once each way around it: a Villarceau circle. Its radius is R, its centre lies in the XY plane at distance r from the origin, and its plane is tilted by θ/2. These values are checked to 10⁻⁸ in `tests/fibration.test.mjs` by fitting circles to projected fiber points.

Moving the pole (the studio's *projection pole tilt*, a rotation of the (x₀, x₃) plane before σ) is a Möbius change of picture: fibers stay circles, or become lines exactly when they pass through the pole, and the tori become Dupin cyclides in general.

## 2. Linking numbers (exact for polygons, numerical for the integral)
Any two distinct fibers are linked once: the linking number of two fibers with the circle-action orientation is +1 in the orientation used here. The studio computes it two ways.

- **Exact crossings.** Fibers are approximated by inscribed N-gons, projected to ℝ³ from a pole chosen to maximise the distance to all displayed curves, then projected to a fixed generic plane. Lk is half the sum of signed crossings. For polygons in general position this is an integer invariant; for N = 160 the polygons are isotopic to the circles in the complement of each other, so it equals the smooth linking number.
- **Gauss integral.** The midpoint rule for Lk = (1/4π)∮∮ (dA × dB)·(A − B)/|A − B|³. Observed convergence is second order: halving the step divides the error by about 4. This is a numerical check, not a proof.

Linking in S³ does not depend on the projection pole, provided the pole avoids both curves.

## 3. The maps F_{p,q}(z₁, z₂) = [z₁ᵖ : z₂^q] (established)
Write F = h ∘ g with g(z) = (z₁ᵖ, z₂^q)/|(z₁ᵖ, z₂^q)|. A generic point of S³ has exactly pq preimages under g, all positively oriented, so deg g = pq. For g: S³ → S³ the Hopf invariant satisfies H(h ∘ g) = deg(g)·H(h), so

  H(F_{p,q}) = pq.

For θ strictly between the poles, F⁻¹(θ, φ) lies on the torus where |z₂|^q/|z₁|ᵖ = tan(θ/2) (the left side is strictly decreasing in |z₁|, so the torus is unique) and is cut out by q arg z₂ − p arg z₁ ≡ φ (mod 2π). With g = gcd(p, q), p = gp′, q = gq′, its components are the curves s ↦ (r₁e^{iq′s}, r₂e^{i((φ + 2πk)/q + p′s)}), k = 0, …, g − 1, each a (p′, q′) torus knot: unknotted when min(p′, q′) = 1, a trefoil for {2, 3}, a cinquefoil for {2, 5}. Two distinct parallel (p′, q′) curves on a standard torus link p′q′ times, so each component of one preimage links each component of the other p′q′ times and the total is g²p′q′ = pq, as the Hopf invariant requires. The test suite checks pq for nine (p, q) pairs by exact crossings and agrees with the Gauss integral to within 0.05 at N = 360.

## 4. Context (established, not computed here)
π₃(S²) ≅ ℤ, generated by h and detected by H. Adams (1960) proved that maps S^{2n−1} → Sⁿ of Hopf invariant one exist only for n = 2, 4, 8, realised by the complex, quaternionic and octonionic Hopf fibrations S³ → S², S⁷ → S⁴ and S¹⁵ → S⁸. The studio visualises only the complex case.

## Limitations
The 3D views are perspective renderings of stereographic ℝ³ with segments beyond |coordinate| > 9 clipped; fibers near the south pole therefore appear truncated. Displayed linking numbers come from polygons, and the Gauss value is a discretisation. None of this is evidence for TUFT or for a physical interpretation of the fibers.
