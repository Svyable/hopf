01 / HIGHER-ORDER MICROSCOPE

What Fourier analysis cannot see.

Edit a sign function on F₂ⁿ. Compute every Walsh coefficient and the exact Gowers U² and U³ power sums, with integer numerators and denominators. Noise flips signs independently with a reproducible seed.

02 / GOWERS CUBE

Touch the eight-point correlation.

Select a base point x and three directions. Each vertex is a binary XOR combination. The product of the eight signs is one term in the U³⁸ average, including degenerate cubes.

03 / INVERSE PROBLEM

Find the hidden polynomial.

For n≤5, exhaustively search all quadratic Boolean phases up to a global sign. Return the largest absolute correlation and compare with the best affine phase. This is a finite exact oracle, not a proof of the general inverse theorem.

04 / ADDITIVE ENERGY

When a set refuses to grow.

Take A={x:f(x)=+1}. Compute A+A in F₂ⁿ, the multiplicities r(s)=|{(a,b):a+b=s}| and the exact additive energy E=Σr(s)². The Cauchy–Schwarz lower bound is |A|⁴/|A+A|.

05 / COMPUTATIONAL WORLD BRIDGE

Is reachability an additive structure?

Map the reachable words from the Rule Space Workbench to +1 and all unreachable words to −1. The same Fourier, cube and sumset tools measure the resulting subset. A binary encoding is a modeling choice, not a canonical invariant under arbitrary relabeling.

06 / EXHAUSTIVE EXPERIMENT

All 65,536 Boolean functions.

On F₂⁴, enumerate all sign functions and group them by their exact U² fourth-power numerator. Each bucket retains a witness. Look at the entire finite landscape, not a few hand-picked examples.
