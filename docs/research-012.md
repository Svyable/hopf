# Research 012 — Temporal information, causal filters and anytime-valid evidence

Inspired by Dean Radin and Julia Mossbridge's predictive anticipatory activity (PAA) research. Mossbridge et al. (2012) report a small positive meta-analytic effect across 26 reports; expectation bias, analysis flexibility, selection and replicability remain disputed. Transparent Psi Project and the 2025 AMP-TPP replication reported negative results for a related Bem precognition paradigm, not a direct test of every PAA protocol. No endorsement, experimental validation or literature-priority claim.

## 012-A. Acausal filtering manufactures a pre-event signal
Synthetic target Y∈{−1,+1}. Ordinary response to a target at t=0 is A Y h(t), h(t)=1_{t≥0} exp(−t/6). Let a linear filter at t have coefficients c_j applied to values X(t+j). Its signed target contrast contribution, 2 E[Y X_filtered(t)], is 2 A Σ_j c_j h(t+j), because Y²=1. For a causal filter j≤0 and t<0 this is identically zero. For a centered box filter j∈[−w,w], the contribution is 2A/(2w+1) Σ_{j=max(−w,−t)}^w exp(−(t+j)/6), nonzero whenever −w≤t<0 and A≠0. This proof concerns the modeled response alone; carryover can contribute separately when targets depend on history. In the UI raw and filtered signed contrasts are twice the empirical mean YX (not the class-mean difference in an imbalanced realization).

The simulator generates an AR(1) noise process with coefficient .8 and innovation SD .6, plus previous-target carryover c Y_{i−1}. All pre-target noise is generated before drawing Y_i. The target probability p_i is .5 initially, then r or 1−r depending on the previous sign, with r=P(repeat). Ordinary response occurs after t=0. The explicit hypothetical alternative adds βY_i exp(t/6) for −12≤t<0 after the draw; this is intentional synthetic leakage standing in for an alternative, not a mechanism or discovery. Data are in sample units, not measured physiology. A bounded score is tanh of the mean over t=−4,...,−1, using either trailing or centered equal-width averaging. Centered scores read post-target values when w≥1; do not treat their evidence as valid. Even causal offline preprocessing can leak via fit parameters, global normalization or target-aware trial selection; those are excluded here by construction.

## 012-B. Conditional likelihood-ratio theorem
Let F_i be the information just before target i, including all previous observations and the current frozen score s_i∈[−1,1]. Under H0, P(Y_i=+1|F_i)=p_i∈(0,1), known by design. This allows adaptive allocation and arbitrary physiological serial dependence. Choose a predictable forecast q_i=p_i+λ s_i min(p_i,1−p_i), |λ|≤1. The one-step factor is q_i/p_i if Y_i=+1 and (1−q_i)/(1−p_i) otherwise. Conditional expectation equals q_i+(1−q_i)=1, and it is nonnegative. The product E_n is a test martingale from 1. A fixed equal-weight mixture over λ∈{−.9,−.6,−.3,−.1,.1,.3,.6,.9} is also a test martingale. Ville's inequality gives P_H0(sup_n E_n≥1/α)≤α. No iid assumption about scores and no fixed stopping time required. The score and forecast must be fixed before the target; wrong p or future-derived features invalidate the theorem. Log-space computation avoids overflow. The exact-null tree routine enumerates all 2^n binary histories for a predictable score equal to the preceding target; it sums their known null probabilities, terminal expected mixture, and crossing probability. This checks a finite example, not the general proof.

The geometric bridge to notebook 011: a classical two-component phase vector z=(1,e^{iθ})/sqrt(2), with reference rays u±=(1,±1)/sqrt(2), gives s=|u+†z|²−|u−†z|²=cos θ. This is a bounded, global-phase-invariant witness suitable for the same theorem if frozen before a target. The simulated physiological score uses tanh instead; mapping it to θ=arccos(s) is a mathematical embedding, not evidence of a quantum brain.

## 012-C. The information limit
For true conditional target probability r, the expected base-2 log factor is r log2(q/p)+(1−r)log2((1−q)/(1−p)) = D_KL(Ber(r)||Ber(p))−D_KL(Ber(r)||Ber(q)). Maximum is the first divergence, attained at q=r. If p=P(Y=1|history) is the true conditional marginal and r=P(Y=1|history,score), averaging the oracle term equals conditional mutual information I(Y;score|history). Under an audited randomized null r=p, it is zero. If p is misspecified, nonzero growth can reflect mundane predictability. This bounds the expected log evidence of a forecast, not a physical communication channel or capacity of consciousness.

## Empirical protocol — proposed, not performed
Predefine sensor, channels, causal filtering, trial baseline, scoring rule, training split, primary target, direction/mixture, alpha and stopping. Freeze features and code before target draw; timestamp and hash commitments in an append-only independently witnessed log, then draw via independently audited unpredictable randomizer, record conditional propensity and target, avoid clock/seed/device leakage. Use benign stimuli, consent, ethics approval, exclusion rules determined without target access, explicit missing-trial handling, no post-event preprocessing or peeking. Include no-participant sham, synthetic leak canary, event-matched control and held-out independent replication; publish null findings and audit code. Multi-site protocols should model shared dependencies and keep one primary e-process or precommit a mixture across tests. A crossing is evidence against a well-specified randomization null, not proof of retrocausality; audit timing, leakage, randomization, data quality and replication before interpretation. No real experiment has been executed.
