# Research 003 — Representation branching and anomaly constraints

All fields are left-handed Weyl fermions, with y=6Y and Q_em=T3+Y. SU(5) is a comparison model, not asserted to be physically realized or uniquely implied by Hopf geometry.

## 003-A — Branching
The standard SU(5) embedding uses the generator diag(-2,-2,-2,3,3). The antisymmetric 10 splits into (3bar,1,-4), (3,2,1), (1,1,6) in integer-y convention. The conjugate 5 splits into (3bar,1,2) and (1,2,-3). A singlet N^c has y=0 in the Standard Model. These give one family, not three. This is textbook grand unification representation theory.

## 003-B — Anomaly and Yukawa constraints
For each field with dimensions d3,d2 and fundamental quadratic indices normalized to 1: SU(3)^3 coefficient is d2 A3 (A3=1 for 3, -1 for 3bar); SU(3)^2 U(1) is d2 T3 y; SU(2)^2 U(1) is d3 T2 y; U(1)^3 is d3 d2 y^3; mixed gravitational is d3 d2 y. The Witten SU(2) global test counts doublets with color multiplicity modulo two. These are the tests for this restricted field content; additional global anomalies or other gauge groups are not covered.

Let q=y_Q, h=y_H. Ordinary Yukawa terms Q H u^c, Q H† d^c, L H† e^c, optionally L H N^c, and SU(2)^2 U(1)=0 imply (q,-q-h,-q+h,-3q,3q+h,3q-h). Direct substitution gives zero SU(3)^3, SU(3)^2 U(1), SU(2)^2 U(1), and Witten parity for all q,h. With N^c, gravitational and cubic anomalies also vanish identically. Without N^c, they are h-3q and (h-3q)^3. Thus h=3q without N^c; with it there is a two-dimensional linear family (hypercharge and B-L). This is a restricted ansatz, not a classification of all gauge theories. The normalization q=1 is an input, not derived; the neutral sterile fermion in the observed Standard Model corresponds to h=3q.

For G=(SU(3)×SU(2)×U(1))/Z6, compatibility is 2t+3s+y=0 mod 6, where t is triality and s is doublet parity. For all fields and the Higgs in this family this is equivalent to q=1 mod 6 and h=3 mod 6. This restricts the integer lattice without selecting a unique point. B-L is a distinct generator and need not satisfy that specific quotient with this normalization. Local anomaly cancellation does not prove global consistency for arbitrary matter.

## 003-C — What remains
Find a geometric selection principle for the global gauge group, the embedding, matter representations, and generation number. Anomaly cancellation is necessary, but neither enough for realistic dynamics nor a mechanism for choosing exactly three identical anomaly-free families.
